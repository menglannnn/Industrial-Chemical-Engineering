# Boots, skirt junctions, flange bolts, manways: photo survey

Batch `2026-10-15-custom-equipment-rendering` · 2026-10-16 · for `EQUIPMENT_DETAILS_PLAN.md` · research subagent (opus), game-level, proportionate. Numbers in [n] are the sources at the end.

## A. Water boot on a horizontal drum

- Place: at the end opposite the inlet, by the liquid outlet, as near the tangent line as the saddle allows [1]. Photos: on the bottom centreline, between the saddles, just inboard of the outlet-end saddle [P1][P2][P3]. Saddles stand at 0.2 L or less from each tangent, 120° wrap, about 0.3 m wide [5].
- Size: diameter at most 0.5 D, at least 0.3 m (D < 0.9 m), 0.4 m (0.9–1.4 m) or 0.5 m (larger) [1]; some cap it at D/3 above 1.5 m [9]. Length from the shell's bottom at least 0.9 m [1][2]; photos 0.3–0.4 D wide, 0.5–0.7 D long. Dished bottom [P1][P3].
- Nozzles: a draw-off at the bottom; a level-instrument pair on one side (interface low 0.2 m above the boot bottom, span at least 0.5 m) [1][3]. No vent.
- Support: the boot hangs from the shell with no support of its own [P1–P3]. Bottom nozzles clear the saddle by at least 0.15 m [3]. Boot drums stand visibly higher, saddles about 0.8–1 D [P3]; the height follows the pump's NPSH or the valves under the drum [3][4].

## B. Skirt to bottom head

- The skirt is welded to the bottom head, often skirt ID = shell OD, flush, the knuckle hidden [7][8][P4][P5].
- Vents: at least two 50 mm holes 180° apart as high as possible (four on larger skirts), often sleeved [10][11].
- Access opening 0.4–0.6 m, sleeved; pipe openings sleeved [7][10].
- Fireproofing: about 50 mm of concrete on the outside over the full skirt height (inside too for D ≥ 1.35 m), bevelled top under a steel flashing at the junction [12][13][P6].
- Seen from 5–20 m: the concrete or brick skirt against the shell with a flashing band, the dark access hole, the base ring and anchor chairs. 50 mm vents and the knuckle do not read.

- A nut is about one bolt diameter high and the stud stands one or two threads past it: about 1.2 d beyond each flange's back [16].

## D. Manways

- Usually 24 in (0.6 m), at least 18 in; under half the vessel's diameter; a vessel of 0.76 m or less gets a body flange, a small one an inspection hole [1][7][17]. A 24 in blind cover weighs about 200 kg, so it hangs on a davit [17].
- Vertical drum: on the shell 0.76 m above the bottom tangent, facing the access side; a platform when more than 3.5 m above grade [3][4][18].
- Horizontal drum: on an end head with the davit above the neck [P1][P3], or on top of the shell [17]; a hinged cover swings sideways [P9].
