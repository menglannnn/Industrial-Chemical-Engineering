## Block break: held networks (owner decision 2026-10-15, implemented)

No removal is dropped: `FluidDeviceBlock.onRemove` queues it for every server-side removal. A queued topology event applies only when each island it fences reaches the fence tick. A held or parked island never reached it, so a removal (or a control edit) next to it waited as long as the hold lasted. IRL, breaking a pipe of a stuck network changes the network at once. The owner decided on both propositions ("let a held or parked island accept a queued topology event at its committed tick (drop the unsolved slice, fresh replacement island), and fence only neighbours that join the edited device"), and they are implemented in `7ae9b66b` and `7036b53d`:

1. **`IslandCoordinator.acceptAtFence`.** It applies only to an island whose last attempt was held, with nothing in flight and no suspension. The island's clock moves to the fence without a solve, the way a certified island materialises; its inventory stands still, so mass and energy are exact. The topology change then replaces the island. The fence gives the island one fresh attempt first, because the edit may free it. If that attempt holds too, the event applies on the next tick. Counters: `heldEventAcceptances` and `droppedHeldTicks`.
2. **`PhysicalRegistry.submit`.** A neighbour is touched only if the edited position joins it before or after the edit. A neighbour that is not joined (for example, two tanks side by side) is no longer fenced.
3. A property hold (`propertyHold`) still freezes all topology events until the data is restored. This is by design.

Tests: `IslandCoordinatorTest.aHeldOwnerAcceptsAFencedEventAtItsCommittedTickWithItsInventoryUnchanged`, `PhysicalRegistryTest.anEventDoesNotFenceAnAdjacentIslandItIsNotJoinedTo` and `PhysicalRegistryTest.aPipeBrokenInAHeldNetworkLeavesItAtOnce` (on the real event path: a removal in a network whose every solve holds applies within two ticks, with the tank mass unchanged).
