## Physics first
- **CO2 in cryogenic liquids** (pilot package `createcheme:pilot_cryogenic`, bundled, selected by a datapack override of `networks/default.json`). Subcooled LNG (139 K, 1.14 MPa, 2.5 % CO2) entering an empty tank flashes at constant enthalpy into the slack bag at 1 atm: boiling LNG at 112.1 K with 19.3 % vapour; the CO2 above its solubility freezes as dry ice at the tank's next refresh, about 5 s later. A liquid-full LNG tank beside 68 kg of dry ice at its bubble point does not boil: its pressure rises. Liquid CO2 let down into an empty 1 bar tank flashes to vapour and about 48 % dry ice at 194.6 K, and the valve stays open (gate D23-1 at 4 mm: 184.8 K between refreshes, 101.3 to 101.5 kPa, 2.0 kg of dry ice after 20 s). Frost forms at 0.25 mm and slush grows to about 1 mm at half solids, where an outlet blocks; solids ride the liquid, so gas leaves a frosted tank and the frost stays.
- **Water ice and data ranges.** Free water below its melting line (273.1526 K at 1 bar, 272.418 K at 10 MPa) is ice Ih. Water reaching LNG at 111.6 K freezes completely (sublimation pressure 5.6e-12 Pa), gives up 12.0 kJ/mol from 20 °C and boils 1.46 mol of methane per mol; humid nitrogen (1170 Pa of water) cooled to −10 °C keeps 260 Pa and loses 78 % of its water as frost; water let down to 100 Pa boils, then freezes at 252.8 K (83 % ice); nitrogen over water in a rigid vessel, given the energy of −10 °C, freezes on the melting line (273.1522 K, 105.5 kPa, 19 % ice: the ice's larger volume compresses the gas), and pure water with only its own steam stands at its triple point, 273.16 K and 611.657 Pa, until its liquid is gone. A material does not change at the end of a data table: CO2 dissolved in LNG far below its 216.59 K triple point, liquid methane below 90.69 K and gases past their ideal-gas fits keep smooth properties (value and slope continuous at the edge) and the island's status line notes it; numerical guards (a 10 to 6000 K, 1 Pa to 1 GPa box, a lost EOS root) reject Newton trials instead, and holds remain only where the model cannot represent the state (`LIQUID_LIQUID`, `THREE_PHASES`, `CRYSTAL_PHASES`, `CRYSTAL_STEP`, the critical band).
- **Columns, lifts and vanishing phases** (the M5 follow-ups). A liquid trace at a tank's bottom port runs down at once and its drop line holds gas: M5 seed 322's 1.8e-8 m3 of condensate over a 2 L line stands as 44.9 Pa of gas over 2 m, where the code had read the trace's liquid density over the whole line and found no root (D34). A gas weighs M g/(Z R T) of its pressure per metre: 11 Pa/m for air at 1 atm, but 0.5 to 1.15 kPa/m for the cold package's CO2-rich gas at 5.4 MPa, 25 to 30 % of an LNG column, so the weightless gas column of D36 was reverted (D38). Holding a 10 m water column costs a pump g Δz = 98 J/kg; with no potential energy in streams that work arrives in the lifted water as heat (+0.0235 K), and a falling liquid no longer warms (0.0094 K per 4 m) (S1); a pump's shaft work is q v Δp and a compressor's its isothermal work, without the efficiency the GUI always set to 1 (S6); a liquid's sound speed (900 to 1500 m/s) never binds under the 100 m/s cap (S5). A bubble in a subcooled liquid condenses and a drop in a superheated gas evaporates: M7 seed 30's re-minted LNG junction, flashed to 4.5 % vapour at 2.03 MPa, returns to 3.49 MPa all liquid (rule C); a failed Newton iterate is not a state, and M8 seed 430's "third fluid phase at 17.35 K" was one, in a bag that cannot cool below about 110 K (D39); a boiling tank draining 2 m into an empty bag delivers liquid exactly at its bubble point under S1, so the 3.4e-6 bubble the check proposes and the next pass drives to nothing is refused (M8 seed 48, D39 B).
