## World 1: `aLiquidTraceAtATanksBottomPortLeavesItsDropLineAGasColumn` (D3: held at tick 744 on the wall deadline)
- **Physics.** A methane generator (431 kPa, 357 K) feeds the upper 1 m3 tank. It also vents through two junctions into 1 atm voids, and those 0.1 m vents really choke (pressure ratio 4.2): about 5 kg/s each at G* = 0.667 ρ c_T, against 1.8 kg/s at 100 m/s. The upper tank (about 425 kPa, holding a crude-condensate trace) fills the empty lower tank 2 m below through 1 m of 0.05 m drop line. That line chokes at 1.27 kg/s while the lower tank is below 0.55 of the upper pressure (0.44 kg/s at 100 m/s). So 1.9 kg goes in about 2 s (4 to 5 s at 100 m/s). At rest the lower tank sits at the upper's pressure plus the line's gas column (about 45 Pa), nothing flows, and the upper tank is dry.
- **What D3 did.** Junction 4 holds 1.6e-8 m3 of condensate (1e-5 of its mass). D3's bulk rule, `liquidVolume+waterVolume>0 ? min(100 m/s, acoustic) : gas choke`, gives it 100 m/s. The pass that folds the trace (D38 C, `folded=[0,1,…]`) gives it 284 m/s, so its vent cap jumps from 1.82 to 5.20 kg/s and the junction drops 4 kPa. The next step brings the trace back. The drop line flips the same way (0.436 ↔ 1.267 kg/s), because a priority mixture takes the smallest limit of its phases, here the trace liquid's 100 m/s. Every step alternates between the two branches (state change 0.031, 0.012, then 0.063 rejected), so the step stays between 0.017 and 0.036 s. After 444 attempts (116 rejected) the 2 s wall budget runs out.
- **Mechanism: an artefact of how the law is wired**, not the higher caps themselves. Whether a trace is present changes the cap by the ratio of the two limits, 2.85×. D2 has only the min over phases: under D2 world 1 does not hold (t38 recorded a hold), but it rests on the wrong column (see the last bullet).
- **Proposition (D3c, in the thermo/transport stage; one statement replaces two rules).** A stream's limit is the throat share of its phases, `V/(V_gas/v_choke + V_condensed/v_max)`, bounded by the mixture's isothermal acoustic bound; that bound keeps the baseline's two-phase bulk limit, for example 28.6 m/s for boiling LNG. A priority mixture's cap gives each pinned phase the area its capacity needs at its own limit, and the marginal phase gets the rest. The limit is continuous in every phase amount and equals today's forms wherever all limits are equal. World 1 no longer holds: 297 attempts, 34 rejected (baseline 311 and 44).
- **What remains: the drop line's column at rest (a pre-existing rule).** Under D3c and D2 the world reaches true rest: the choked vents settle the upper tank at 357 K. On the baseline the upper tank is still cooling at the end tick, which keeps +9e-9 kg/s flowing down the line. The line then joins a cold, heavy gas above (44.9 Pa over 2 m) to the warm lower tank's light gas (426 K, 37.5 Pa). Both rests lie on the boundary of `columnFromFirst`, whose inclusive tests pick the other column there. The lower tank chatters ±7 Pa (±1.5e-5 kg/s), and the test reads 37.5 Pa where it expects 44.9. In a real line this density inversion exchanges gas by counterflow, and the static difference sits between the two columns.

## World 2: `MixedGasJunctionTransientTest …WithinTwiceTheMeasuredCost` (3 of 12 cases over their bound)
- **Physics.** In the 20 mm cases a 1 m3 methane tank at 150 kPa and 350 K (0.83 kg) blows down through about 1 m of 20 mm line into a junction at 101.6 kPa. The pressure ratio, 1.48, is below the critical 1.83, and friction lowers the flow further. An isothermal Fanno estimate gives 0.054 to 0.064 kg/s, 75 to 85 % of the choke flux, and the tank reaches 102 kPa in 4 to 5 s. The 100 m/s cap held the flow at 0.026 kg/s, making it a 10 s linear blowdown.
- **What D3 did.** Over bound: 0.02:4:true 37 Newton solves (bound 32), 0.02:5:true 38 (34), 0.02:6:true 41 (38); all twelve cases 396 against 299. In 0.02:4:true the law flow is 0.066 to 0.071 kg/s, so the whole 150 → 102 kPa blowdown falls in the first 5 s interval. That interval takes 14 step attempts. Two are rejected at h = 1 and 0.5 s (state change 10 and 5.3 % against `STATE_CAP` 5 %), then h grows about 1.07 per step as the drive decays. The capped run took 5 + 5. Newton iterations per attempt are unchanged (8.7 against 9.2), so the extra work is not in the cap's pressure derivative or the junction buffer (3.7 g against 1.3 g, 0.05 s lag).
- **Mechanism: a real consequence of the higher caps.** Faster, more physical transients meet the 5 % state-change control. `BackwardEulerStepTest controlledIntervalAgreesWithFixedSteps…` fails the same way: its nitrogen blowdown no longer runs at a constant capped flow, and the controlled interval lands 2.35 % from 256 fixed steps against a 1.5 % tolerance (0.88 % under the cap).
- **Proposition: restate and keep the gate.** The references become the measured choke-law counts (396 in all), with the bound still twice the reference. `BackwardEulerStepTest`'s tolerance becomes 3 %. This is not the mechanism behind the bundled +17 % (below).

## World 3: `aFilterBetweenTwoWanderingJunctionsConverges` (held under D2; passes under D3 and D3c)
- **Physics.** A methane generator (490 kPa, 372 K) feeds two empty tanks and a 1 atm void through 10 mm lines and a filter. Gas flows are 0.01 to 0.03 kg/s, and the junctions sit between 101 and 490 kPa.
- **Why D2 and D3 differ.** D2 gives the choke to phase-port gas streams only. So in this island the empty tanks' gas ports draw at 204 m/s while every junction (bulk) end stays at 100 m/s: one gas gets two caps depending on the end type. The baseline and D2 start from the same junction seeds, 102 and 476 kPa either side of the filter. The baseline's first 0.05 s step converges in 20 Newton iterations; D2's exceeds the limit of 30 at all 20 step sizes. D3 gives every gas end its choke. Its fixed-point start reads the same caps and seeds the junctions at 120 to 300 kPa, and the first pass converges in 8 iterations. D2's split by end type is the defect; nothing to propose beyond D3 or D3c.

`VelocityClampTest aWarmWorkspaceReleasesTheClamp…` is a restatement, as the brief says: the configured 1 m/s no longer binds a gas. At the 290 m/s choke the law flow is only 4.2 times the cap, so the flow sits 1.25e-6 below it where the test asks for 1e-6. Restate the test on a liquid line.
